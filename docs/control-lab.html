Three jobs, working together

Profile: decide where the arm should be at each moment.

Feedforward: predict the motor effort needed for gravity, speed, and acceleration.

Feedback: correct the difference between the reference and what actually happened.

Read the three lines

Destination is the final angle. Profile target is the controller’s reference right now. Actual is the measured angle.

Without a profile, the reference jumps to the destination. The arm does not jump—it still has to move. With Motion Magic, the reference follows a trajectory.

Why no integral slider?

Integral, kI, accumulates error and can remove a persistent final offset. It can also keep accumulating when the mechanism cannot move: windup. We leave it at zero while learning feedforward and feedback.

Motion profiling does not replace integral. Accurate feedforward can reduce the residual error that would otherwise motivate it. A real mechanism may still finish slightly off target.

What does D do?

For position control, D responds to velocity error: reference velocity minus actual velocity. At a stationary target it acts like damping. During a profile it can also add effort if the arm is moving too slowly.

Voltage-controlled mechanisms already have some natural damping. Zero D can be a good result. Torque-current control is different and often needs more D; this lesson stays with voltage control.

About this model

The model includes inertia, gravity, motor back EMF, a ±12 V output limit, and physical travel limits. It intentionally leaves out friction, sensor noise, and delays. That is why we do not add a kS friction slider.

CTRE’s Motion Magic generates the profile. It does not automatically identify the feedforward gains. Our browser profile is a teaching approximation, not a reproduction of Phoenix firmware.
