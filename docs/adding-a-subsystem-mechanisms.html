Mechanisms

Explore how motor rotation becomes mechanism movement.

Which mechanism are you building?

Showing the Elevator instructions below.

The motor (labeled M) turns a series of gears. Those gears turn a drum, which winds in a cable and lifts the elevator. Same-colored parts share a shaft and turn together: each time the blue gear makes one full turn, the blue drum also makes one full turn. Press Play to see how many motor turns it takes to lift it 1 meter, or drag the slider.

How many motor turns for 1 meter?

  1. Gears: 4 × 3 = 12 motor turns per drum turn. This is the gear ratio: motor turns per mechanism turn.
  2. Drum: its radius is 0.025 m, so its circumference is 2 × π × 0.025 ≈ 0.157 m. One turn winds in that much cable and raises the elevator by the same distance.
  3. Put them together: 1 m ÷ 0.157 m per drum turn ≈ 6.37 drum turns. Multiply by 12 to get about 76.4 motor turns.

The motor (labeled M) turns a series of gears. Same-colored parts share a shaft and turn together. The purple arm is attached to the last purple gear, so they rotate together. Press Play to see how many motor turns it takes to raise the arm through 90°, or drag the slider.

How many motor turns for 90°?

  1. Gears: 4 × 4 × 5 = 80 motor turns per arm turn. This is the gear ratio: motor turns per mechanism turn.
  2. Angle: 90° ÷ 360° = ¼ of an arm turn.
  3. Put them together: ¼ × 80 = 20 motor turns.

Remember these relationships in code

A constant is a named value that stays the same while the program runs. The starter saves these values in Constants.java:

  • GEAR_RATIO = 12.0: motor turns per drum turn. It relates motor rotation to drum rotation.
  • METERS_PER_DRUM_TURN: meters of elevator travel per drum turn. Our code uses it to convert between drum turns and height.

The second constant is calculated as 2.0 * Math.PI * DRUM_RADIUS_METERS. We use the calculated circumference in code, not the rounded 0.157 shown above.

GEAR_RATIO = 80.0 saves the number of motor turns per arm turn. Our code converts between arm turns and degrees using 360 degrees per turn.
